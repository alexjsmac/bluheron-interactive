Strathroy Immersed brought Museum Strathroy-Caradoc's local history off the
wall and into visitors' hands. The museum wanted an exhibition that young
people would help make, not just visit, so the content came from a Grade 10
history class at Holy Cross Catholic Secondary School, and BluHeron's job was
to turn their research, writing, music, and animation into exhibits a visitor
could play.

That meant teaching first. Alex worked with the students on creative coding
and digital storytelling, then planned the exhibit with the museum: which
stories became which stations, how each one would be handled by a visitor who
walks in cold, and what had to be true for it to survive a summer of museum
hours.

The stations are deliberately simple to pick up. In *Dance Dance Arthur*,
visitors step along to the students' own music through five scenes from Sir
Arthur Currie's First World War; there's no score and no failing, and every
round ends with the students' text about the scene. In *The Turning Year*, a
physical clock hand chooses a month and brings back that month's image and
sound. Sliders, a keyboard, and other hands-on controls drive the rest, each
one a different way into the same town's history.

The part visitors never see is the part the museum needed most. Everything
runs from a single show computer that starts itself, recovers on its own if
anything stops, and shuts down at night, so staff never have to troubleshoot
software between visitors. Their whole job is turning it on.
