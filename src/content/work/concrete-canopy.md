For Nuit Blanche London 2025, Concrete Canopy transformed the historic
Kingsmill's Department Store façade into a living digital organism — a place
where architecture and nature merge in an evolving audiovisual symphony.
Vegetation climbed the stone, flowers opened across the window bays, and the
building appeared to be reclaimed by the growth it had displaced.

The piece was participatory by design. Rather than playing a fixed film at the
crowd, it handed the controls over: a custom web application put visitors in a
queue on their own phones, and each person in turn got thirty seconds to shape
the visuals and the generative soundscape in real time. Nobody watched the same
show, because the show was being made by whoever was standing there.

That turn-taking is the argument. A projection this size is usually something a
city does *to* a street; this one only worked if strangers were willing to line
up, take a turn, and hand it on. The tension between built and natural
environments was the subject on the wall — collective responsibility for the
shape of a city was the thing happening on the sidewalk.

Concrete Canopy was both visual poem and interactive playground: an invitation
to listen, engage, and reimagine how we inhabit our cities.
