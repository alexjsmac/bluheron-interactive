Jump Around is a disco game show, and it is also a public consultation. The City
of Hamilton's Public Art Department needed community input for its Public Art
Master Plan, and the usual instruments — surveys, open houses, comment forms —
mostly reach the people who already turn up to City processes.

So we put the questions inside a party. Developed with creative collaborator
Colina Phillips, Jump Around brings people of every age and ability onto the same
dance floor and into the same game, and the rounds of that game are the
consultation. Starting from artistic practice buys moments of joy and surprise,
and those moments reach people who would never fill in a form.

The [results](../../jump-around-public-art-results.pdf) went back to the
department in full. Eighty per cent of respondents said art in public places
enhances their connection to their community, and only seven per cent said they
barely notice it. Asked what kind of public art they wanted more of, the largest
group — forty-one per cent — chose live performance and interactive art, ahead
of permanent sculpture. Asked which community values matter most, kindness and
compassion came first.

The write-in answers are the part worth keeping. A checkbox tells you which
option won; the sentences people volunteered — decolonization, third spaces,
support for individual Hamilton artists, more Indigenous art and representation
— are the ones that change what a Master Plan should say.
